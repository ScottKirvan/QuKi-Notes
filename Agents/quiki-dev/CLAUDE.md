### Selection Stage 3 — auto-scroll while dragging a handle

**Branch**: `feat/selection-stage3`
**Commit type**: `feat:` — genuinely new capability.
**Suggested PR title** (Spec opens the PR, not you): `feat(editor): auto-scroll while dragging a selection handle (selection stage 3)`

**Read first**: `notes/dev/selection.md` §4 ("Auto-scroll during drag") — the authoritative target spec. Also read `notes/dev/decisions.md` → ADR-36 in full for how Stages 1 and 2 landed, especially Stage 2's two fix rounds — this stage sits directly on top of the exact machinery those rounds fixed, and both bugs are worth understanding before you touch this code, not just as history. Then read the current handle implementation yourself: `packages/markdown_live_editor/lib/src/quiki_editor.dart`'s `_buildSelectionHandlesOverlay`, `_onStartHandlePanStart/Update/End`, `_onEndHandlePanStart/Update/End`, `_sourceOffsetForGlobal`, `_ActiveHandleDrag`, `_handleOverlayTick`/`_onScrollChangedForHandles`, and `selection_handle.dart`.

**Context you need, not background color**: Stage 2 shipped only after two real-device-driven fix rounds, both about the exact same category of problem this stage adds more of — the relationship between scroll position, screen-to-text coordinate resolution, and where a handle actually renders. Round 1 fixed a real bug where the handle overlay's rebuild-after-scroll could read a stale paint transform (fixed via deferring the rebuild trigger through a post-frame callback — `_handleOverlayTick`). Round 2 fixed a real bug where the raw touch position wasn't corrected for the handle's own paint-time vertical offset before being resolved to a text position (fixed in `_sourceOffsetForGlobal`). Auto-scroll means text is now moving underneath an actively-dragging finger *continuously*, not just once between drag gestures — every one of those prior landmines is still live here, now happening on every scroll tick instead of once. Do not treat this as "just add a scroll timer" — the position-resolution correctness this stage depends on is exactly what took two rounds to get right for the simpler, already-shipped case.

**What "correct" means here** — the confirmed requirement from `selection.md` §4:

- While actively dragging a handle, if the pointer's current position comes within some reasonable threshold distance of the top or bottom edge of the visible scrollable viewport, the content must begin auto-scrolling in that direction, continuously, for as long as the pointer stays in that zone — not a single one-shot jump.
- Auto-scroll must stop as soon as the pointer moves back away from the edge zone, and must not scroll past the start or end of the document.
- Throughout the auto-scroll, the selection must keep extending correctly to whatever text scrolls into view under the finger — this is the actual hard part, not the scrolling mechanism itself. The resolved text position has to stay accurate on every tick, not just once scrolling stops (which is exactly where Stage 2's Round 1 bug lived — a rebuild that read a stale transform right after a scroll change).
- This lets a selection extend to content that wasn't on screen when the drag started, without the user needing to lift their finger, manually scroll, and resume dragging.

**Explicitly out of scope for this stage**:
- No magnifier, no haptics — Stage 4.
- No changes to how a selection is *created* (long-press, double-tap, `_selectEntityAt`) — out of scope, unaffected.
- No changes to desktop/mouse click-drag-select — unaffected; auto-scroll-while-dragging is a touch-handle-specific affordance, same scoping logic Stage 2 already established for handles generally.
- No changes to the single collapsed-cursor handle — still not built, still not this stage's job (unchanged from Stage 2's scoping).
- Do not touch the toolbar-hide-during-drag behavior from Stage 2 unless auto-scroll genuinely requires it — if you find you do need to change it, explain why rather than changing it incidentally.

**Required test coverage — real gestures, not programmatic selection**, same discipline as every prior stage: simulate a drag that holds position near a viewport edge across multiple ticks (not just a single instantaneous check) and assert both that scrolling actually happened *and* that the resulting selection is correct throughout, not just at the end — a test that only checks the final state after auto-scroll settles could hide exactly the kind of transient-staleness bug Stage 2's Round 1 needed a real device to catch. At minimum, cover: holding a drag near the bottom edge with more content below extends the selection into content that wasn't visible when the drag started; holding near the top edge with more content above does the same in reverse; auto-scroll stops when the pointer moves back away from the edge; auto-scroll doesn't scroll past the actual start/end of the document; the handle's own rendered position stays visually correct (not stale) throughout continuous scrolling, not just after it stops.

**Checklist**: `dart format`, `flutter analyze`, both package tests (`cd packages/markdown_live_editor && flutter test`) **and root tests (`flutter test` from the repo root)** — both suites, every time, no exceptions, given this exact area's history of a root-level miss slipping through once already. Report back: the actual auto-scroll mechanism you chose and why, confirmation (with test evidence, not just a claim) that the selection stays correct throughout continuous scrolling, and anything about the interaction between auto-scroll and the existing `_handleOverlayTick`/post-frame-callback machinery from Stage 2 Round 1 that concerned you or required a judgment call. This is a real-device-dependent feature by nature (continuous touch-and-hold timing, scroll physics) — say plainly what you could not verify without one, and do not claim confidence you don't have.
