# Why QuKi Notes Works This Way

QuKi Notes makes some deliberate choices that may feel unfamiliar if you're used to other note apps. This page explains the reasoning behind them.

## The editor is always home

When you open QuKi Notes, you land in a blank editor. There's no list, no dashboard, no recent-files screen. The editor doesn't have a back button.

This is intentional. The most common action is capture: you have a thought and you need to write it down now. Every extra screen between opening the app and a cursor is friction. The editor removes that friction by being the starting point, not a destination you navigate to.

If you want your previous QuKis, the list is one tap away. It just isn't the default, because capture is the default.

## No folders, tags, or organization

QuKi Notes has no way to put QuKis into folders, tag them, link them to each other, or archive them. This isn't a missing feature; it's a deliberate constraint.

Organization takes time. Deciding where something goes, maintaining a taxonomy, keeping it consistent: these slow down capture and turn a note app into a productivity system. QuKi Notes isn't a productivity system.

Most QuKis don't need to go anywhere. Sometimes a thought just needs somewhere to live: off your mind, available if it ever turns out to be useful. If something does need to live somewhere specific, send it there.

## Your data is yours

On Android, Windows and Linux, every QuKi is a plain-text `.md` file in a folder on your device. There's no proprietary format and no cloud account between you and your own notes. You can read, copy, edit or move the files without opening the app, and any `.md` file you drop into the folder shows up as a QuKi.

The web version is the exception for now: it keeps QuKis inside your browser, where other programs can't reach them (see [Settings → Storage](/user-guide/settings#storage)).

## Deleting takes two steps

Deleting a QuKi moves it to Trash; it doesn't erase it. From Trash you can restore it, delete it for good, or leave it. Anything left in Trash for 30 days is permanently deleted the next time QuKi Notes starts.

QuKis in your list never expire. The newest sit at the top and older ones drift down, but they stay until you decide they're done.

## Sending

The Send button is there when a QuKi needs to go somewhere: the share sheet on Android, the clipboard on Windows and Linux. It's always something you do; QuKi Notes never sends your QuKis anywhere on its own.

A QuKi that never gets sent is fine. It stays available, searchable and editable for as long as you want it.
